## Preview, update and reset safely

**Manage sales demos** first displays a read-only blueprint: one dedicated fictional company, eleven prepared identities, eight issued role logins, the company business day, represented features and useful non-success states. Expanding **Roles and feature coverage** explains which people receive no portal login. Reviewing the blueprint performs no provisioning, seeding, messaging, financial writes or audit creation; it remains available without an identity-provider connection. Confirm the blueprint before building.

Fresh builds/resumes/rebuilds require the declared development/test environment and configured demo identity capability. A blueprint-bound setup captures application origin, identity issuer, fixture revision and business day. Those values are rechecked before provider work and before the business graph commits. Changed environment, fixture or business day does not silently rebase an unfinished operation: retire the owned unfinished setup and review a new blueprint. Previously reviewed older build blueprints expire against fixture7. Existing completed generations keep their original dates and history. Legacy setups without a stored blueprint retain checked recovery/retirement compatibility; their ownership is still checked.

Use **Presentation walkthrough** on a generation to inspect its retained source links, roles and date anchor. Sign in with that role before opening a source. The guide does not grant platform owners company access, impersonate another account or enable provider features. Missing older-fixture sources are visible through coverage rather than invented as successful records.

Use **Review fixture update** or **Review fresh rebuild** to update/reset the presentation. The plan explains the existing generation, affected company members, new fixture and retained history. Confirmation reserves one successor with a durable submission key, retires the original through the existing lifecycle, then builds a new dedicated company and new accounts. Posted cash, leases, documents and audits stay attached to the old company. A repeated request resumes/returns the same replacement; it cannot create another replacement with the same key. A partial unique database guard allows only one active replacement per source generation, while retaining abandoned/retired successor provenance.

If retirement or source creation fails, resume the reserved replacement under **Unfinished setups**. A provider cleanup failure leaves old local access disabled; no replacement business graph is created before cleanup completes. If the original changes between reservation and execution, no old account is disabled by that attempt: retire the unfinished replacement and review the original again. A combined rebuild has a bounded six-minute browser/request budget, with the same original-request recovery rules after response loss. Do not discard an uncertain review or replace its key.

**Tear down demo** now previews the current action before confirmation. Source/company/member/identity changes invalidate the reviewed plan before writes. An unrelated account added to the demo company blocks retirement/rebuild entirely, so that account, membership and sessions are not disabled. The review now names additional members and generated accounts with changed authority, saved email, company assignment or issuer/subject. Unfiltered platform Users links keep moved accounts discoverable; a separate Support link opens the exact platform grant. Remove only a separately added member's company access through the existing versioned Company access → No company access controls if appropriate, and review/revoke unintended Support grants separately. Company ownership or residency does not grant platform authority. Refresh the action plan after corrections; both mismatch reasons and exact identities are included in its hash. Accepting a generated pending tenant's invitation is an owned membership and does not become a foreign-member blocker. A generated account reassigned elsewhere also blocks cleanup. Teardown is retirement, not hard deletion: source and audit history remain available. A legacy presenter is never blocked in Auth0; company-qualified cleanup preserves any subsequent customer-company membership/session.

The UI always supplies a reviewed blueprint/action hash. For legacy compatibility, direct create and teardown API calls may omit that hash; supplied hashes are validated. Rebuild strictly requires the reviewed hash and explicit confirmation. Hashless creation captures the current environment/fixture/day before provisioning; hashless teardown requires the current demo version and freshly validates exact identity/company ownership, foreign members and the operation lease. Both retain platform authorization and CSRF checks. The compatibility limit is review evidence: without a hash, teardown does not prove that the caller saw the current company/member snapshot. It operates on the freshly validated state. Operator tooling should follow the reviewed workflow.

Cleanup deliberately does not require today's fixture/day or provisioning to remain enabled: interrupted owned identities must still be retired. Blueprint-bound cleanup requires its saved environment name, application origin and identity issuer to match. Before claiming teardown or changing local access, the reconciliation also checks any known saved account issuer/connection and exact local Identity issuer, including pre-blueprint rows. Known mismatch returns `409 DEMO_PROVIDER_CHANGED` without company/account/session changes, provider calls, retirement or cipher clearing. Genuinely unknown historical issuer/connection context retains a compatibility limit: it cannot establish original context that was never saved. Exact local subjects and generation ownership markers remain required; ordinary/customer companies and unrelated identities remain outside cleanup.

## Recovery and teardown

Auth0 calls run outside database transactions. Before any provider write, the server durably records the generation, submission identity and encrypted credentials. A short database transaction claims a bounded five-minute operation lease; each provider step renews it. Exact generation markers recover provider success after a lost response. Fresh platform authority is checked between steps. A failed source/audit write rolls back the entire business graph while keeping the setup and owned identities available for retry.

Confirmed prepared-account progress records both provider issuer and database connection. Resume skips a confirmed identity only when both match the current provider. Older issuer-only or unbound receipts are verified once through the paced provider and upgraded without regenerating passwords or replacing the encrypted credential payload. Known saved/local context mismatch is rejected before effects rather than interpreted as account absence in another tenant.

If setup is interrupted, retry the original form or select **Resume demo setup** under **Unfinished setups**. Completed retries return the same generation and passwords. Concurrent setup/teardown returns a useful busy conflict. After a process crash, a lease can take up to five minutes to expire before retry is allowed. A stale browser cannot silently create a second copy through the original submission.

**Tear down demo → Confirm teardown** first passes the saved-context and ownership preflight, then suspends the demo company, disables its generated local users/memberships and revokes company and support sessions atomically. It then blocks only the exact marked Auth0 identities. If Auth0 is unavailable after local retirement starts, local access stays disabled and **Teardown needs retry** remains visible. Retry finishes provider cleanup, including an ambiguous create whose subject was not yet recorded. Cleanup uses saved owned addresses, or the pinned fixture email format for an ambiguous create, and remains possible if the credential encryption key is unavailable. Unfinished manual-password attempts also block cleanup with an account review link; resolve/release the exact attempt through its password controls first. Successful teardown removes the encrypted credential payload. Source, cash, document and audit history is retained. Build a new demo for fresh accounts/data; an old teardown cannot affect it.

An identity-service busy response (`429`) means Auth0 limited a provider request. Keep the same unfinished setup and wait for the displayed retry interval before confirming teardown again. The client connection applies a one-second demo pacing floor, respects later provider retry/reset times and rejects early retries during longer cooldowns without another provider request. It never automatically retries identity-creation POSTs. Each confirmed block/absence is acknowledged durably against that exact generated subject; issuer/connection metadata survives retries and 429 responses. Interrupted cleanup skips confirmed subjects and continues with the remaining ones. Unknown subjects still require exact generation-marker lookup. A fresh local ownership callback precedes each block operation; durable subject reservations prevent reactivation or reassignment throughout provider waits and interrupted retries. A separate **Ready** demo is another generation and is outside this cleanup.

One absolute 110-second provider-work budget spans the operation's token/coalesced waits, FIFO queue, pacing, HTTP fetch and JSON response work. A rebuild shares that budget across old-generation cleanup and replacement account preparation; it does not grant a new 110 seconds to each account or phase. Expired queued work cannot execute later. Deadline failure returns typed `503 IDENTITY_PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` with the safe `deadline` category and retains exact durable progress for explicit retry. The five-minute DB operation lease and six-minute combined browser/request budget have different purposes; neither grants an unlimited provider retry loop.

Provider failures distinguish throttling, authentication/permission rejection, outages, transport/deadline failure and unreadable responses. Request logs include only fixed provider operation/failure categories, whether a rate limit came from the upstream response or a local cooldown, numeric upstream status when a response exists and bounded retry seconds, correlated with the request ID; raw provider bodies, URLs, emails, tokens, passwords and connection secrets are excluded. Pacing is per server process/connection; other application tasks or Auth0 clients can share the tenant quota. Queue/pacing work consumes the shared provider deadline. Missing configuration or provider permissions require administrator correction; repeated cleanup cannot grant those permissions. Local/stub checks do not establish that live quota, permissions or delivery have been corrected.

Exact generated subjects in a pending or retiring setup remain reserved until the operation finishes. Platform reactivation, company access changes, support enablement and invitation grants return a conflict while that reservation exists. This prevents a generated identity becoming a customer account between the committed local ownership check and the external Auth0 block. The reservation survives an expired operator lease or provider failure; retry cleanup to release it. Provider HTTP never holds a database transaction open. Ordinary non-demo users and legacy presenters have no such reservation.

A generated account moved to another company or given platform authority blocks credential reveal and teardown until its assignment is reviewed. Those actions never change a customer's assignment or identity. Version-one demos remain listed for retirement: their existing presenter account is released and preserved, rather than disabled in Auth0. Review a fresh version7 rebuild for automatic role logins and the current presentation guide.

## Credential protection and maintenance

The existing `AUTH0_WEB_CLIENT_SECRET` derives a separate AES-256-GCM key through HKDF with a demo-specific purpose. Random nonces and the generation ID as authenticated data prevent cross-generation substitution. Passwords are encrypted in `SalesDemoSetup.credentialsCipher`; they appear only in authenticated, CSRF-protected, freshly authorized, audited POST responses with `Cache-Control:no-store`. Lists, audits, telemetry and provider DTOs exclude passwords. App/client secrets are never returned. A manual administrative password change invalidates the original role-login sheet before provider dispatch; reveal rejects it instead of returning an obsolete password. Successful/reviewed password changes do not change generation ownership, and teardown still uses exact saved accounts. Initial generated passwords may also stop working if changed separately in Auth0; rebuild to reset the demo.

Retain the application secret while existing encrypted credentials are needed. Rotating it makes older credential payloads unreadable; restore the prior secret or tear down/rebuild. Teardown can block known demo identities without decrypting passwords. No plaintext passwords, dumps or credential artifacts belong in Git or diagnostics.

Keep the seed, `DEMO_FEATURE_COVERAGE`, `DEMO_PRESENTATION_STOPS`, blueprint, walkthrough and role-access regressions current as features change; bump `SALES_DEMO_SEED_VERSION` for fixture changes. Never upgrade posted demo history in place. The no-login examples have no company membership or issued password; account preparation alone never grants portal authority. Fixed browser operations remain `sales_demo.create`, `sales_demo.teardown` and `sales_demo.credentials`; rebuild uses the create operation with a longer bounded deadline. Source/setup/reveal/retirement/rebuild-reservation audit events contain safe identifiers only, never plan payloads, hashes, credentials or contact contents. Standard request failure/duration monitoring covers the platform endpoints. Use `node scripts/test-sales-demo.mjs` for disposable API/browser validation, `server/tests/sales-demo-plan.test.mjs` for pure planning contracts and `tests/ui/sales-demo-overhaul.spec.ts` for focused UX. These checks simulate Auth0 and do not establish live provider or deployed acceptance. The internal disposable-test marker is set only by the guarded runner; it is not an operator shortcut for enabling demos in another environment.
